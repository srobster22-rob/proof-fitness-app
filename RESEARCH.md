# Market scan — strength logging apps (July 2026)

## Who owns the market today

| App | What it wins on | Free tier | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hevy** | Best free tier + in-app social feed. The default recommendation on Reddit. | Contested across sources: some report unlimited routines/history, others report 4 routines / 3 months history / 7 custom exercises. Verify in-app before relying on it. | $2.99/mo, $23.99/yr, $74.99 lifetime |
| **Strong** | Fastest pure logging UI, best per-exercise analytics | 3 routines | Paid PRO |
| **Boostcamp** | 130+ coach programs (5/3/1, nSuns, GZCLP) + 11,000 community routines | Generous | Paid tier |
| **Fitbod** | AI-generated sessions | Thin | Subscription |
| **Knurl** | **The closest competitor by far.** No accounts, no sign-in, no server, iCloud sync, PR timeline, e1RM charts, goals vs logged lifts — and logging from the Lock Screen / Dynamic Island via a Live Activity, without unlocking the phone. | n/a | n/a |

## What users actually complain about

The single most repeated complaint across Reddit threads: **too many taps to log a set.** Lifters want to log in 2–3 seconds and get back under the bar. Secondary complaints:

- Can't instantly see "what did I do last week on this machine" when standing at the rack
- Apps that force their own programming model onto people running a named program
- Deep hostility to paywalls on *core* features

Notably, **graphs and social features rank below speed** in what users say they want. This is important: it means a prettier dashboard is not a wedge.

## Monetization reality

- Fitness free→paid conversion is typically **2–5%**
- Health & Fitness has the **highest trial-to-paid rate of any category (~35%)** but the **lowest day-1 retention (30.3%)** — people bounce fast, so the first session has to land
- Hard paywalls convert ~5.5x better on day-35 download-to-paid (12.1% vs 2.2% median) — **but freemium yields 15–25% more total revenue over 12 months** because it captures late converters
- H&F is the only category where **annual plans dominate** (60.6% of revenue)

Implication: the "no signup, no trial, no paywall" instinct is defensible — it costs short-term conversion but wins on 12-month revenue and retention, and it is the only way to beat Hevy's free tier on first impression.

## The actual gap

Sharing is an **afterthought** in every serious logger. Hevy's "shareables" are static image cards of volume/PR/muscle distribution — you still assemble the post yourself. Boostcamp and Strong are worse. The closest thing found is **VideoTimer** (1 set = 1 video, hands-free recording during rest) — but it records sets for *review*, it does not produce a postable highlight.

Meanwhile the auto-vertical-video tooling (OpusClip, Reap, Media.io, BlitzCut) is a **separate app category entirely** — general-purpose editors that know nothing about your lifts.

**Nobody joins the two.** No app makes "a clip worth posting" the natural byproduct of logging a set.

## Why that gap is worth money

It is not just a feature — it is a customer-acquisition strategy. Every competitor pays for growth. If a shareable clip is the default output of the core loop, users' own posts become the ad channel, watermarked, on the platform where gym content already performs enormously.

## Caveats worth holding

0. **Knurl occupies the "no account, local-first, fast" position already**, and logs from the Lock Screen without unlocking the phone. Anything in this document that treats zero-friction onboarding as an opening is out of date — that ground is taken. The gap below is the only one left.
1. The logging market is **saturated and commoditised**. Competing on "simple fast logger" alone is a loss — Hevy already won that on price (free).
2. The gap may exist because it is hard, not because it is unnoticed. Video rendering on-device is the real technical risk.
3. Most people who film sets already film them — the behaviour exists. That's the encouraging signal.
